Propagating committee proofs: four strategies compared

leansim experiments on branch feature/subnet-proof-strategies · updated 23 November 2026 · Shadow simulations of 16 committees on 3,713 hosts

Summary. Every proof of every committee has to reach every host. We compared four ways of spreading 200 kB proofs: announce and pull, the whole proof on GossipSub, signed Merkle pieces on GossipSub (EIP-8411 style), and the same pieces on Waggle. Waggle was the fastest at the 95th percentile in all six matched cases and the only strategy with every host ready within 4 s. Announce and pull sent the fewest bytes, 2% above one copy, and trailed Waggle by 0.19 s with four proofs per committee. Pieces on GossipSub were the slowest and the heaviest.

1. What we simulate and what we measure

Each run is one round of proof dissemination. It starts when the proofs exist: these runs send no signatures and do no proving.

  • Workload. 16 committees. Each has 1, 2 or 4 producers, so a run carries 16, 32 or 64 proofs. Every producer releases its own 200,000-byte synthetic proof at the same instant, and nothing is sent ahead of that instant. The proofs of one committee differ from each other, and every one of them is meant to reach every host. Producers are distinct hosts holding validators of their committee; the roster for one producer per committee is the first round of the roster for two, and so on.
  • Coverage. Every host became ready in every run. All runs but two delivered every proof to every host. The two runs of pieces on GossipSub ended 3 and 1 deliveries short of 237,568.

Metrics

  • Ready: time from the release until a host holds at least one complete proof for every one of the 16 committees, which is what a proposer would need before it can combine them. A host's own proof counts. Median, 95th percentile and last host, over all 3,713 hosts.
  • Ready by 4 s: how many of the 3,713 hosts were ready 4 s after the release.
  • MB sent per host: bytes written to QUIC streams, averaged over hosts: proof data including duplicates, signatures, framing and control messages. It leaves out UDP/IP and QUIC packet overhead and retransmissions, so it is not wire bytes.
  • × one copy: the same bytes divided by one transfer of every proof to every other host, which is 3.20, 6.40 and 12.80 MB per host for 16, 32 and 64 proofs. A strategy that delivers every proof cannot go below 1.

The clock starts at the release, not at the start of a slot. These numbers do not show by themselves that a slot schedule holds.

2. The four strategies

  • Announce and pull. A host that holds a complete proof announces it to all its connected peers. A peer requests the proof from the first announcer, keeps one request open per proof, and tries another announcer if the request fails. It announces only after the whole proof has arrived, so each hop adds a full 200 kB transfer.
  • Whole proof on GossipSub. The producer publishes the proof as one message on a global proof topic. Mesh peers forward it when they hold all of it. IDONTWANT is enabled for messages above 1,000 bytes.
  • Pieces, in both piece strategies. The producer cuts the proof into 64 pieces of 3,125 bytes, builds a Merkle tree over them and signs only the root, with one 2,400-byte signature per proof. Every piece travels with its 192-byte Merkle path. A receiver recomputes the root from the piece and the path, verifies the producer's signature over that root once per proof, and holds the proof to it. There is no erasure coding: a host needs all 64 pieces of the same producer.
  • Pieces on GossipSub. The producer publishes the pieces as 64 separate messages on one global topic, and every message carries the signature, because a publisher cannot know which receivers already hold it. A piece message is 3,125 + 192 + 2,400 bytes. A host verifies a piece before it forwards it. This adapts the chunked GossipSub path of the EIP-8411 draft.
  • Pieces on Waggle. The pieces travel on Waggle streams between connected hosts, with no GossipSub involved. A host adds the signature to a piece only while the peer may lack it: it leaves it out once the peer has advertised or sent a piece of that proof, or once it has queued the peer a signed piece. A receiver refuses a piece without a signature while it holds no signed root. A host pushes each piece to at most two peers, picking those with the shortest send queues. A relay pushes only during the 250 ms after its first verified piece; the producer always pushes. Hosts advertise the pieces they hold every 50 ms. From 100 ms after it first hears of a proof, a host requests missing pieces, rarest first, with one open request per peer per proof. A request that is not answered within 550 ms goes to another peer. The policy sits in the application; the vendored Waggle library is unchanged.

Waggle had the lowest 95th percentile in all six cases, and the shortest tail. Its 95th-percentile host was ready after 1.85, 2.10 and 3.10 s with one, two and four proofs per committee, and its last host after at most 3.75 s. No other strategy had every host ready by 4 s in all of its runs.
  • Announce and pull is the cheapest, and it gets faster with more proofs. It sends 2.4% more than one copy of each proof. Its 95th percentile falls from 3.72 to 3.28 s as producers are added, probably because a host needs only one proof per committee and more producers put one closer; we did not measure hop counts. With four proofs per committee its median host (2.12 / 2.15 s) is ahead of Waggle's (2.66 / 2.66 s), its 95th percentile is 0.19 s behind, and it sends 43% fewer bytes.
  • Waggle's lead shrinks as proofs are added. Its 95th percentile grows by 1.25 s from one to four proofs per committee, while announce and pull improves by 0.44 s. The gap between them goes from 1.87 s to 0.19 s. We did not test more than four.
  • The whole proof on GossipSub sends about seven times the one-copy bytes. 22.8, 45.8 and 91.4 MB per host, and the 95th percentile rises from 3.10 to 3.78 s. With four proofs per committee the last host needs about 8 s.
  • Pieces on GossipSub are the slowest and the heaviest. With four proofs per committee the 95th-percentile host needs 11.03 / 11.22 s and the last host 19.0 / 19.3 s, within a second of the end of the run. Only 764 and 656 of the 3,713 hosts are ready by 4 s, and a host sends 118 MB.
We report the result of pieces on GossipSub for this setup: 64 pieces, the signature in every piece, four proofs per committee, the mesh-4 setting, simultaneous release. We have not run it with fewer proofs or at the mesh-8 setting, and we do not attribute its loss to a particular mechanism. Section 5 shows how much of it the signature accounts for.

4. How many bytes each strategy sends

  • Announce and pull: 1.024. A host fetches each proof once, so nothing arrives twice. The remaining 2.4% is announcements, requests and framing.
  • Whole proof on GossipSub: 7.1 to 7.2 at the mesh-8 setting, with IDONTWANT enabled. We did not measure the mesh degree of the proof topic or the copies per mesh link in these runs, so we cannot say how the excess splits between mesh forwards and gossip.
  • Pieces on GossipSub: 9.21 at the mesh-4 setting. A piece message is 5,717 bytes for 3,125 bytes of proof, so one copy of every piece already costs 1.83 times the proof.
  • Waggle: 1.67 to 1.81. The Merkle paths add 6.1% to every piece. The signature rides on 11 to 14% of the piece messages, which is 11 to 13 signed messages sent per host and proof, and makes up 7 to 9% of the bytes. The rest of the excess is pieces pushed to a host that already held them, inventories and requests. We have not instrumented how it splits.

For scale, a 50 Mbit/s uplink moves 6.25 MB per second. With four proofs per committee the whole proof on GossipSub sends 91 MB per host on average and pieces on GossipSub 118 MB, about 15 and 19 s of such an uplink, against 2.1 s for announce and pull and 3.7 s for Waggle. The strategies that send the most also have the longest tails, which is what we would expect if uplinks are the bottleneck. We infer this from the totals; we recorded neither queues nor per-host bytes, and the hosts with 1 Gbit/s likely send more than the average.

5. What the root signature costs

Two more piece formats were run on the same network, to see what the 2,400-byte signature does and where it should travel.

  • Pieces on GossipSub without the root signature. Each piece carries the Merkle root, and the producer's ordinary pubsub message signature covers it. A piece message is 3,349 bytes instead of 5,717.
  • Waggle with the signature in every piece. 16 pieces of 12,500 bytes, each with the root, the signature and its Merkle path, so that the signature is 19% of a piece and not 77%.

6. A smaller GossipSub mesh

In the signature runs the mesh size was the largest single lever on duplicates. For proofs we ran the whole proof with four proofs per committee at both the mesh-8 setting (low 4, target 8, high 12) and the mesh-4 setting (low 3, target 4, high 6). The connections, placement and producers are the same; only the three mesh settings differ. The rows are in the last group of the table in section 3.

The mesh-4 setting saves about a fifth of the bytes and does not make the whole proof competitive. It sends 21% less, its 95th-percentile host is 0.53 s later, fewer hosts are ready by 4 s, and it still sends more than five times the one-copy bytes.

Pieces on GossipSub with the signature were run at the mesh-4 setting only. For the unsigned format of section 5 the mesh-4 setting sent 19% fewer bytes than mesh-8, and its 95th percentile was 6.48 / 6.90 s against 6.82 / 6.88 s.

7. Conclusion: the best setup so far

Move authenticated pieces on Waggle: 64 pieces with their Merkle paths, the signature over the root sent to a peer only while it may lack it, a short push followed by rarest-first requests.

# proof and pieces (campaign flags)

PUSH_WINDOW       = 250 ms   # a relay pushes only this long after its first verified piece

REQUEST_DELAY     = 100 ms   # earliest request after a host first hears of a proof
QUEUE_RETRY       = 100 ms   # retry after a send that failed
request timeout   = 550 ms   # 500 ms + 16 µs per piece byte; then another peer

Waggle buys the tail with bytes. Against announce and pull it brings the 95th-percentile host forward by 0.19 s and the last host by 1.78 s, and sends 77% more. Against the whole proof on GossipSub it is faster and sends about a quarter of the bytes.

Variants

  • If bytes matter more than the tail, use announce and pull: within 2.4% of the one-copy floor, and its median host is ahead of Waggle's with four proofs per committee. With one proof per committee its 95th percentile is 1.87 s behind Waggle.
  • If the proofs must stay on GossipSub, send them whole. With the signature in every piece, pieces took about three times as long at the 95th percentile with four proofs per committee.
